Your data, explained

Privacy policy

Effective date: November 12, 2026

Quiet Shelf is a free, open-source reading tracker created by Alex (Ayca Kaygusuz). You can use it without an account. Google sign-in and Google Drive sync are optional.

This policy covers the official Quiet Shelf apps, the local browser version, and this project website. Independently modified copies and third-party hosting may have different practices. The official source and releases are linked from the home page.

The essentials: reading records stay on your device unless you export them or enable sync. With sync, a copy goes to your own Google Drive app-data folder. Quiet Shelf does not request access to your other Drive files, and does not sell your data or use it for advertising.

1. Local data and backups

Quiet Shelf stores the information you add to your library on your device: titles, authors, edition details, ISBNs, notes/descriptions, library/archive/wishlist placement, reading statuses, start and finish dates, rereads, series, and related book metadata. It also saves preferences such as theme, sorting, view options, sync/update settings, and the latest Read next checklist and exclusions.

The browser version uses browser storage for library data. Native apps store library data in their application profile. Signed-out and Google-account workspaces are kept separate. Local recovery copies are created before the first-sync choice changes a workspace; the latest three recovery sets are kept on that device.

JSON backup/export includes library records and supported preferences. It excludes Google sign-in tokens, account identity, connection credentials, and the optional Google Books API key. Exported files are not encrypted by Quiet Shelf: store and share them carefully. Restoring a backup into a signed-in workspace can make those restored records part of its synced library.

2. Google account and Drive data

If you choose Sign in with Google, Google handles authentication. Quiet Shelf receives a Google account identifier, verified email address, and available display name to identify your account, display the active connection, and keep account workspaces separate. Quiet Shelf does not receive your Google password.

The app requests basic identity permissions (openid, email, and profile) and https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata. The Drive permission allows access only to Quiet Shelf’s own hidden app-data folder, not your other Drive files.

After you approve how to connect your local library, sync reads and writes library records, reading histories, series, supported preferences, cached book metadata, Read next settings/checklist, deletion markers, and conflict information in that folder. This data is used to keep your chosen devices in sync and resolve concurrent edits. The first connection checks Drive read-only and asks whether to start with, combine, or use the available library before changing the workspace.

Google account identity is handled separately from library backups. User authorization tokens and Google Books API keys are not included in Drive library snapshots. Google processes sign-in and Drive storage under Google’s Privacy Policy. Quiet Shelf has no developer-operated central service that receives your library for native or local-browser sync.

Limited Use: Quiet Shelf’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used to provide account connection and library synchronization. It is not sold, used for advertising, used to train AI models, or shared with book catalogs. The Read next selector runs in the app and does not send your library to a generative AI service.

3. Credentials and security

OAuth tokens authorize the app to access the permitted Google data. Native credentials are handled by native code and platform storage, rather than exposed to library page scripts. Windows encrypts remembered tokens using OS-backed storage; Linux remembers them only when an accepted secure keyring is available. Without one, Linux sign-in is session-only. Mobile authorization uses Google’s native SDKs and platform credential mechanisms.

The local browser version keeps remembered authorization in encrypted, HttpOnly cookies with a locally generated server key. A separately hosted browser deployment would also require a server to handle OAuth and encrypted session cookies; this project website is not such a deployment and does not process sign-in.

Network requests use HTTPS, apart from local communication on your own device. Library profiles, JSON exports, and Drive library snapshots are not end-to-end encrypted by Quiet Shelf. Anyone with access to your device profile, exported files, or Google account may be able to access the corresponding records. No storage or transmission method can be guaranteed completely secure.

4. Catalog searches, cover images, and updates

Searching a catalog sends the entered query, ISBN or catalog link, and selected language filter to the chosen provider through the app’s catalog adapter. Opening a result can fetch edition details. Read next metadata lookups occur only when you request generation or refresh; they can use saved edition links and ISBNs with the original provider, Open Library, or Google Books.

Available sources include Kitapyurdu, BKM Kitap, Nadir Kitap, Open Library, Google Books, DNB, K10plus, Thalia, Hugendubel, LovelyBooks, Blackwell’s, Better World Books, and AniList. Cover images may be loaded from their image hosts. These providers receive the requested search or resource and ordinary network information, such as the requesting IP address and request headers. Their own privacy policies apply. Following an external source link opens that third-party website.

If you provide an optional Google Books API key, Quiet Shelf saves it separately on the current device and sends it only for Google Books requests through its adapter. It is not part of your library, JSON backups, or Drive sync. Clear its field in Add title → Google Books to remove the saved key.

Update checks contact GitHub for public release information; requested downloads contact GitHub’s download hosts. These requests do not contain library records, your Google identity, user OAuth tokens, or your Books API key. GitHub receives ordinary request/network information. Automatic checks can be disabled in Account → App updates; manual checks remain available. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.

The official app contains no advertising or analytics SDK for reporting your library or usage to the developer. If you choose to submit a bug report or support request, the information you include is shared with the service used for that report.

5. Retention, deletion, and your choices

  • Use it without Google. Local tracking and JSON backup/restore work without signing in.
  • Pause sync. Turn off automatic sync or sign out in Account. Signing out removes that installation’s active connection; it does not delete local libraries, recovery copies, or Drive data, or sign other devices out.
  • Revoke Google access. Remove Quiet Shelf under your Google Account’s third-party connections. This prevents further authorized access but does not itself guarantee deletion of existing app-data files or local copies.
  • Delete library records. Delete entries in the app. With sync enabled, deletion markers are shared so other devices can apply those deletions. Conflict records, recovery copies, previously exported backups, and older checkpoints may retain information until separately removed or superseded; deleting one entry is not a full account erasure.
  • Remove Drive app data. First disconnect Quiet Shelf on your other devices to prevent re-upload. In Google Drive’s web settings, find Manage apps → Quiet Shelf and use the option to delete its hidden app data. Google controls this interface and storage retention.
  • Remove local data. Clear Quiet Shelf’s app/browser storage or remove its local profile, and separately delete exported backups or recovery downloads you no longer want. Windows and Linux uninstallers preserve library profiles, so uninstalling alone does not erase them. Clearing storage can remove unsynced changes.

Local data is retained until you remove it or the platform clears that storage. Drive data is retained in your account until deleted or replaced during normal sync; the app uses checkpoints and deletion markers to protect concurrent changes. Quiet Shelf cannot delete copies you exported, data held by independent third-party services, or files on a disconnected device on your behalf.

6. This project website

This informational site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It has no sign-in, advertising, analytics scripts, cookies, or local-storage features added by Quiet Shelf. Fonts and images are served with the site; it does not load third-party embeds. GitHub may process visitor IP addresses and standard request information to operate and secure its hosting under its privacy statement. Clicking a download or external link takes you to the relevant provider.

7. Contact and policy changes

Quiet Shelf is maintained by Alex (Ayca Kaygusuz). For privacy questions or to request help, use the project’s issue tracker. Issues are public: do not post your library export, email address, credentials, tokens, or other private information there. You can ask for a private contact method without including those details. The Google consent screen also lists the configured user-support email.

This policy will be updated if the official app’s data practices change. The effective date appears above, and the source repository records revisions. Material changes that require new Google permissions will require the relevant Google consent.
